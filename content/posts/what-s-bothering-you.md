Information about climate change has an effect on my behaviour and it is not the same effect it has on my family, which causes friction.

I don't want to fly short distances for a short period, for instance. This lead to quite a row between my wife and I. Similar things occur when it is about leaving the lights on, turning on the heat and opening a window, eating meat and (not) separating the trash.

Now I have to think about this: will these little things have an effect on the global warming, or should I realise that these small changes aren't going to c*ut it anyway?

Am I doing these things to make myself feel better or is this really helping to get to a more sustainable future?

Because if you think about it, it would be much easier (not better, just easier) to not be bothered about the small things while still focusing on the big things.  Yes, it would. But practically speaking, what "big things" do you have an effect on? 

Let's focus on the things I do:

- solar panels on my roof

- electric car

- eat less meat

- separate garbage

The problem I see with fighting global warming is that people who are concerned about it, become nagging, unpleasant people in the eyes of other people. As my son put it the other day: I can be a "*pret-spons*", a sponge for fun, absorbing the fun. 

As you can imagine, that hurt. And the thing is, I see what he means. I am being this unpleasant guy, reminding people to turn off the lights, don't fly, don't eat meat, don't leave the window open while the heating is on (or vice versa), buy less stuff. 

So I am making my own family miserable, while the rest of the world can do what they want. That's not very productive, is it? 

OK, current approach is not the way to go, how to change it?

Dropping my concern for a warming planet with all its consequences isn't possible, I'm afraid. Putting things in perspective might help though. My parents raised me with a certain believe that you should do what's in your power to conserve energy. But what is the effect of me not flying? (no, don't tell me "what if everybody thought like that", I'm already aware of that).

How many flights are there everyday? How many people travel how many kilometres? How does that average out? Where do I stand relative to that avarage? 

> *Global energy-related CO2 emissions stood at around 36.44 billion metric tons in 2019*

That is 5.2 metric ton per person (assuming 7 billion people worldwide)

[Worldometer](https://www.worldometers.info/co2-emissions/co2-emissions-per-capita/) has a slightly lower average of CO2 emission per capita, with high differences between different countries.

The Netherlands has a whopping 9.62 metric tons per capita (2016 figures), so how can I calculate my own emissions? Or should I stay away from even wanting to know, and focus on worldwide reduction instead?

I definetely should do the latter, but I want to know what my carbon foot-print is, so heading over to [a calculator](https://www.carbonfootprint.com/calculator.aspx) to figure out what carbon footprint of me and my family is.

After filling out the details (I don't have all the necessary data at hand), it is clear that flying contributes the most to the height of my carbon footprint. Electricity usage is high, higher than it used to be, but that is also due to the fact that we charge two electric cars at home.

Other good news is that we actually use less gas than a comparable household of 5 in a villa. So, lesson 1: stop nagging your family about leaving lights on!
