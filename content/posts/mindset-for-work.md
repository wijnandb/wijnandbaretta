An important thought occurred to me. There are several projects of which I'm thinking of in terms of "*finishing the project*", in a sense that I'm thinking about how all the work has been done and there is nothing left to do. In a way, that is the goal that I have in mind. And I have come to the conclusion that it's a wrong  mindset to be thinking about projects. The reason(s) for why it is wrong are: 
1. it's unrealistic
2. it's contra-productive
3. it gives me stress
4. it costs me money

Instead of focusing on getting a project finished, I should realise that getting to the next step is what matters. Especially if I want to earn a decent amount of money, the focus should be on determining what I will do in exchange for which amount of money.

The big advantage of my of working at the moment, is that I can deliver a working project in every stage of the process. 

I tend to struggle with asking for a specified amount of money for certain work that I do, but I think I have to realise that this isn't a science, but far more an art: a client is willing to pay me money, and somewhere, somehow this has a (perceived) relation with the amount of time I will put in to get it done, but I really have to stop worrying about wheter my asssessment is correct. Far more important is: is the client willing to pay the requested amount? And, am I happy to do the work for that amount?

So, more of my focus should be writing proposals: I can do this for you, for this amount of money. 
And if I don't know how much time or effort it will take to build something, either break it donne and put prices on smaller pats of the deliverable(s), or overprice. They always have the option to either decline the offer or ask for a discount. 

I can put this idea to the test by accompanying the proposal I'm writing for the Brede School with an amount which I will invoice if they agree.
