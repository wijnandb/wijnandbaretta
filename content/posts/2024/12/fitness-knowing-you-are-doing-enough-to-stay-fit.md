I am 55 years old and I do work out occassionally. I have noticed that I like to know what I should do to stay fit. Now there is plenty advice on the most elaborate training programs, but it seems that if you start looking into that, the advice is to keep increasing the amount, the weight and the frequency.

Being 55, I am mainly interested in staying fit andstrond, not sop much getting a lot stronger.
The downside of the elaborate program or extensive training regime, is that it can feel as so much that you feel you can not live up to the expectations and therefor you quit early.

So basicsally I am looking for a way to stay motivated to do the required amount of training, without burning out or feeling I am not doing enough.
Another goal: make it attractive to start working out again after you have lapsed off.

So, what I was thinking about, was measuring (or actually: calculating) the score for each muscle or muscle group.
By breaking down the body in its main muscles (or musclegroups, but from here on out I will refger to them as muscles, ebven if they are actually musclegroups). By calculating a value for each muscle, based on the exercises that you have performed over time, we can give a very targeted advice, which can even be: "You are perfectly fit for a 55 year old. If you want to workout anyway, we advice you to focus on msucle X and Y. They are at Z level. A good exercise would be A or B, with a weight of J for I reps."

Maybe a bit too long, but you get the drift.

OK, so how to do that?

1. Get a list of the different muscles
2. Get a list of potentia; exercises.
3. Define which muscles are trained by which exercises.
4. Create a formula to establish the value for each muscle, based on history of exercises.
5. Visualize the level of the muscles using colors, based on calculated values and age.

1. and 2. and 3. are easy, I have them in a [Google sheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1hMtDPBkoucd7drpoOjBn7Dsq_dM7nNJBWvXijJST024/edit).
